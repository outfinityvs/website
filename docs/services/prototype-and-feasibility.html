What It Delivers

The Prototype & Feasibility Package bridges the gap between a technical idea and an informed build decision. The client brings a concept; Outfinity assembles a small team to build a controlled prototype that demonstrates the core technical approach, documents the architecture, identifies the main risks, and estimates what it would cost in time and money to turn the concept into a production-ready product. The prototype is not a minimum viable product and is not built for end users. It is a technical feasibility instrument: it tests whether the concept works under controlled conditions, reveals architectural choices that affect cost and scalability, and gives the client concrete information for deciding whether to invest in full development, seek partners, or redirect the idea.

The engagement delivers a working technical demo, scoped during the initial planning to focus on the hardest or least-certain parts of the system. The prototype may take the form of a command-line tool, a simple web interface, an API endpoint, or a notebook that demonstrates the critical path. Alongside the prototype, Outfinity provides documented system architecture covering components, data flows, integration points, technology choices, and the rationale behind key decisions. A risk analysis identifies technical, operational, and market risks discovered during prototyping, each described with its likelihood, impact, and possible mitigations. The cost estimation breaks down the time, team size, infrastructure, and external costs required to reach production, organized by phase from MVP through scaling to enterprise readiness. Where relevant, the package also includes a build-versus-buy-versus-partner assessment evaluating whether the capability should be built internally, licensed, or developed through a partnership.

When It Is Useful

This package is designed for corporate innovation teams that have an AI or deep-tech idea but need technical evidence before committing a larger budget, family businesses exploring AI applications for their industry, and technical firms that want an external team to validate a concept before spinning out a separate venture. Research groups with published results that want to understand what it would take to turn those results into a commercial product are also a strong fit. The engagement typically runs 5–7 weeks. The first week is scoping: Outfinity defines the prototype scope, the technical questions to answer, the success criteria, and the deliverables with the client, and a fixed timeline and budget are agreed before work begins. Three to five weeks of prototyping follow, with a mid-point checkpoint to review progress. The final week delivers the prototype, documentation, risk analysis, and cost estimation, accompanied by a 90-minute discussion session to review findings and implications for the next decision.
