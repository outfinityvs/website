What It Delivers

Not every technical question requires a weeks-long engagement. Sometimes an investor needs a qualified second opinion on a startup's architecture claims before a Monday committee meeting. Sometimes a corporate innovation team wants to know whether a particular AI approach is even feasible before writing a project brief. Sometimes a family office considering a co-investment needs an honest conversation with someone who has built similar systems and can explain the real risks in plain language. Technical Advisory is the simplest way to access that expertise: you book time, you ask your questions, and you receive direct, technically competent answers from a senior researcher or engineer who has worked on the relevant class of problems.

The service is delivered in blocks of hours, typically through video calls with screen sharing, collaborative whiteboarding, or code and architecture review as the situation requires. There is no minimum long-term commitment and no retainer requirement. A short email exchange beforehand defines the topic and the expected scope so the time is used efficiently from the first minute. The conversation can cover architecture review, technology selection, feasibility assessment, investment screening interviews, risk identification, cost estimation for technical builds, or any other question where a few hours of focused senior attention can save weeks of misdirected effort or a costly misjudgment.

The advisor assigned will be a senior researcher or engineer with a PhD or equivalent depth in the relevant domain. Outfinity draws on a network that includes people who have built agentic systems, designed privacy-preserving infrastructure, worked on regulated AI pipelines, developed neuro-symbolic architectures, and operated AI platforms at scale. The client is not paying for a junior analyst to research the topic from scratch but for someone who already understands the landscape and can assess the question with the speed that comes from experience. Pricing starts at 250 CHF per hour and reflects the seniority of the advisor, the domain specificity, and the preparation required. Rates are negotiated transparently before the engagement begins, and the client controls the number of hours.

When It Is Useful

Technical Advisory is designed for situations where the client needs a rapid, expert-informed perspective without committing to a full validation engagement. An angel investor evaluating a startup's technical claims before leading a round can use an advisory session to pressure-test the architecture and identify red flags that a non-technical investor would miss. A corporate innovation director who has been pitched an AI solution by an internal team or an external vendor can use advisory time to understand whether the proposed approach is technically sound, whether the claimed timelines are realistic, and what alternatives should be considered before committing budget.

For family offices and multi-family offices, advisory sessions can serve as a technical screening layer before deeper due diligence. A one-hour conversation about a potential co-investment can reveal whether the opportunity justifies the cost of a full Venture Exploration Memo or a dedicated Technical Due Diligence engagement. This keeps the family office's analytical budget focused on the opportunities that genuinely deserve it. Technical firms exploring a new product direction can use advisory sessions to discuss architecture trade-offs, technology choices, and the feasibility of integrating AI capabilities into an existing product, without disclosing proprietary detail until the engagement is formalized.
