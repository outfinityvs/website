Governance and Institutional Impact

We have a particular interest in how blockchain and AI could influence the governance of firms, states, and societies. Distributed ledger technologies, smart contracts, and AI-mediated decision-making open new possibilities for how organisations coordinate, allocate authority, and maintain accountability. We study these shifts not as abstract theory but as forces that will reshape corporate structures, public institutions, and the social contract itself.

Our published research contributes to this conversation. See, for example, our work on blockchain-enabled governance models: Springer, Blockchain and Governance. We continue to explore how decentralised systems interact with existing legal frameworks, institutional trust, and the practical realities of organisational life.

The Decentralised Brand

One concept we are actively developing is the decentralised brand, a model that sits between fully centralised corporate identity and fully decentralised community ownership. As AI-generated content, deepfakes, and algorithmic manipulation erode public trust, the decentralised brand offers a middle path: an identity structure that is anchored by a credible core but open to community participation, verification, and shared meaning.

We see this as a potential response to the impact of AI on society. When synthetic media becomes ubiquitous, trust cannot rely on content alone. It must be anchored in verifiable identity, transparent provenance, and social consensus. The decentralised brand is one way to build that anchor, giving organisations and communities a structure that is resilient to manipulation while remaining adaptable and participatory.

Beyond Social Networks

We are also looking at startups and directions that aim to replace social networks with something more meaningful and more useful. The current social network model, a single undifferentiated feed exposing every psychological impulse to the same public arena, has become socially expensive. It amplifies outrage, flattens identity, and leaves users exhausted without feeling that anything of value happened.

The next generation of social technology may not be another network, productivity tool, or generic metaverse. It may be a curated system of portals into alternative realities: structured digital worlds where people enter not only to be entertained, but to belong, compete, rehearse identity, recover from social exhaustion, and spend surplus cognitive time in environments that feel psychologically meaningful.

As AI absorbs more work, the market will need systems that help people live with the loss of necessary production. A large part of human identity has historically been organised around work, community, ritual, career progression, and visible usefulness. If those structures weaken while people gain additional free cognitive time, the result will not automatically be enlightenment. A portal platform can compete with social networks by offering something stronger than a feed: entry into worlds with rules, roles, memories, rituals, rankings, communities, and consequences.

Areas of Interest

We are interested in a broad class of startups that reorganise digital social life around structured environments rather than open feeds. The categories below describe the kinds of ventures we believe could emerge from this thesis, and the scope of each direction.

Belonging

Small-group belonging platforms

AI-matched communities of bounded size, optimised for trust, continuity, and mutual recognition. These address the loneliness epidemic and the failure of large networks to provide genuine social cohesion. The core challenge is matching, retention, and the design of durable group rituals.

Relationships

Persistent AI relationship systems

Long-lived AI companions, mentors, rivals, or collaborators that accumulate memory, personality, and context over time. These differ from chatbots in that they carry continuity and emotional depth. The commercial potential lies in personalisation, voice, private spaces, and evolving relationship dynamics.

Simulation

Simulation and rehearsal environments

Structured practice for high-stakes human situations: negotiation, leadership, conflict, grief, parenting, interviews, and difficult conversations. These sit at the intersection of training, therapy, and entertainment, and can serve both consumer and enterprise markets.

Competition

Structured competition and debate

Environments that channel ego, aggression, and intellectual rivalry into ranked, moderated, rule-governed performance. Unlike social network arguments, which are unstructured and toxic, these make competition productive and containable.

Knowledge

Deep-interest communities

Organised around obsessive domains where learning becomes identity and status: science, history, strategy, craft, philosophy, or any field where depth of knowledge creates social structure. These replace the shallow engagement of topic-based feeds with genuine intellectual communities.

Production

Productive social environments

Digital settings that recreate the social texture of work: guilds, workshops, collaborative projects, ranking systems, and shared production. These become relevant as AI reduces the amount of necessary labour and people seek structured activity that feels useful.

Identity

Identity and aesthetic platforms

Centred on bodily reinvention, fashion, beauty, avatar design, and social presence as creative practice. These carry strong creator-economy potential and natural monetisation through cosmetics, premium identity tools, and social visibility.

Emotion

Emotional and relational simulation

Exploring courtship, flirting, emotional confidence, and relational rehearsal in controlled digital environments. Demand is large but design sensitivity around consent, dependency, and manipulation is critical.

Impact

Verifiable impact and moral status

Gamified volunteering, civic contribution, and ethical behaviour, giving moral status to useful work. These can support partnerships with NGOs, research groups, cities, and enterprises.

Catharsis

Controlled catharsis environments

Fictional, rule-bound spaces for psychological intensity: destruction simulations, role reversal, moral theatre, ideological experimentation, and symbolic risk. These address human drives that current social networks expose chaotically but that structured environments can contain safely.

The common thread across all these categories is that they replace the undifferentiated public feed with social architecture: bounded environments with rules, roles, progression, memory, and consequences. The core product is not hardware immersion. It is structured social experience, delivered through web, mobile, voice, AI characters, persistent avatars, generated environments, and lightweight interactive scenes. A small room with ten humans, a persistent AI host, evolving memory, status rituals, and a clear social purpose can be more engaging than a technically sophisticated but socially empty virtual city.

We are studying which of these categories have the strongest combination of psychological demand, implementation feasibility, and defensible commercial logic. We expect the first viable ventures in this space to emerge from the categories with the highest retention potential and the lowest technical complexity, expanding over time into more ambitious environments as trust, moderation capacity, and identity infrastructure mature.

Direction

This research direction sits at the intersection of governance theory, social technology design, and venture exploration. We are interested in backing and building startups that address the structural gaps left by current social platforms: verifiable identity, meaningful community, psychological depth, and governance innovation. The venture path may take the form of a portal platform, a decentralised-brand infrastructure service, or governance tooling for organisations navigating the transition to AI-mediated operations.

We are actively looking for researchers, founders, and investors who share an interest in these questions and want to explore them as serious commercial directions rather than speculative concepts.
