Legal and Intellectual Property Principles

Clear rights around code, research, open-source work, and venture assets make collaboration faster and company formation stronger.

Background

Background intellectual property includes code, research, models, datasets, methods, documentation, and know-how that existed before a collaboration or were created independently outside its scope. These assets remain clear and traceable throughout the venture-building process. When background IP becomes important to a venture path, it can be licensed, assigned, or otherwise structured in writing so the original owner and the future company both have clarity.

Contributions

Open-source contributions to Outfinity-designated repositories follow the license attached to those repositories. This creates a transparent framework for shared technical work and reusable infrastructure. Paid work is handled through specific agreements that define the deliverables and the rights needed for the venture, the studio, or the paying entity to use the resulting work effectively. Paid exploration outputs, such as Venture Exploration Memos, Validation Sprint results, and Technical Due Diligence reports, give the client access and rights defined contractually, but any investment, equity rights, or company participation are treated separately through distinct legal agreements.

As a venture matures, the company receives the rights it needs through assignments, licenses, founder contributions, service agreements, or commercial arrangements that match the opportunity. Confidential information shared during validation, partnership, or financing discussions is handled with the same level of clarity. This gives the company a usable and defensible asset base while preserving the reusable technologies that may support additional ventures across the portfolio.

Value

Investors, customers, and strategic partners want ventures that can explain what they control, what they license, and how the company is allowed to operate. Clear rights make that conversation far easier. That is why legal and IP discipline is part of the operating model from the beginning: it strengthens execution, financing, and long-term company value.
