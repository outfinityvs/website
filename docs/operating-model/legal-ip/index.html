Operating Model

Legal and Intellectual Property Principles

Outfinity treats legal and intellectual property clarity as a practical requirement for venture formation, not as paperwork to solve after the collaboration is already valuable.

Operating ModelCollaboration ProcessLegal and IP Principles

Background intellectual property

Background intellectual property includes technology, code, research, models, methods, documentation, and know-how that existed before the collaboration or was created independently outside its scope. Outfinity does not assume that such assets become studio property merely because a conversation or project takes place.

If background IP is important to a venture path, it should be licensed, assigned, or otherwise handled explicitly in writing. That clarity protects both the original owner and the future venture.

Open-source work and paid work

Open-source contributions to Outfinity-designated repositories are governed by the license attached to those repositories. That provides a transparent framework for shared technical work without implying broader ownership transfers that were never agreed.

Paid work is different. When project-specific paid work is commissioned, the rights over that output should be stated clearly in the relevant agreement. The studio prefers explicit terms over convenient assumptions.

Why this matters for venture formation

A venture becomes fragile when it depends on code, methods, or knowledge that nobody has framed clearly enough to use, license, or defend. Rights ambiguity creates real commercial risk, especially when investors, partners, or future acquirers need to understand what the venture actually controls.

That is why Outfinity aims to create legal and IP clarity early. It is one of the foundations of a credible venture, not a secondary polish step.
