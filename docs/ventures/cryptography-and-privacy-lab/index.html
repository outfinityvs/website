Deep pharma experience through OpenDSU

Our team brings extensive hands-on experience from applying and productionizing OpenDSU (opendsu.org) in regulated pharmaceutical environments. OpenDSU provides a secure, traceable data infrastructure that has been deployed across clinical trials, supply chain management, and regulatory compliance workflows in pharma.

This real-world experience with GxP-compliant systems, audit trails, and multi-party data collaboration in highly regulated settings forms the foundation for our approach to privacy-preserving technologies. We understand the practical challenges of deploying advanced cryptography in environments where regulatory approval, patient safety, and data integrity are non-negotiable.

Next-generation enterprise blockchain with AI

We have prepared ideas for new generations of enterprise blockchain systems, designed from the ground up to integrate with AI workloads. Unlike traditional blockchain approaches that struggle with privacy and performance at scale, our architecture combines confidential computing, zero-knowledge proofs, and selective disclosure mechanisms that allow organizations to collaborate without exposing sensitive business logic or data.

These systems are designed for regulated industries where auditability and provenance matter, but where the transparency of public blockchains is incompatible with competitive and regulatory requirements. The architecture supports complex smart contracts that can execute AI inference, aggregate insights across participants, and enforce data governance policies at the protocol level.

Federated learning with AI agents from Project Achilles

Our federated learning approach goes beyond traditional model aggregation. Drawing from the Achilles project, we introduce AI agents that operate within each participant's trusted environment. These agents can execute sophisticated analysis tasks, enforce export policies, and participate in collaborative workflows while keeping sensitive data under local control.

The Achilles agents are designed to work in regulated environments where data cannot leave the organization's boundary. They enable collaborative intelligence that includes not just model training but also analysis, knowledge extraction, and decision support, all while maintaining strong boundaries around proprietary and sensitive data.

Private data management with neuro-symbolic architectures

For managing highly sensitive data such as genetic information, financial records, or healthcare data, we explore neuro-symbolic architectures like Vector Symbolic Architectures (VSA) and Hyperdimensional Computing (HDC). These approaches allow computation on encrypted or obfuscated data representations, enabling analysis without exposing the underlying sensitive information.

VSA/HDC techniques can represent complex data in high-dimensional spaces where operations can be performed without revealing the original data. This is particularly valuable for genetic data analysis, where multiple organizations want to collaborate on research without sharing individual genomic information, or for financial institutions that need to detect patterns across customer data without exposing transaction details.

Advanced Differential Privacy approaches

We are developing new types of Differential Privacy mechanisms that go beyond standard noise-addition approaches. Our work focuses on context-aware privacy budgets, adaptive privacy guarantees that adjust based on the sensitivity of queries, and composition theorems that allow organizations to precisely control their privacy exposure across multiple collaborations.

These advanced approaches enable more useful analysis results while maintaining rigorous privacy guarantees. They are particularly important for longitudinal studies, continuous learning systems, and multi-party collaborations where traditional differential privacy mechanisms would either destroy utility or fail to provide adequate protection over time.

Venture direction and partnership model

The venture path leads toward a platform that enables governed AI collaboration across organizational boundaries, with particular strength in pharma, healthcare, and financial services. Initial customers are likely to be research consortia, hospital networks, pharmaceutical companies, and industry groups that need shared intelligence without centralized data.

Revenue comes from platform subscriptions, deployment services, and consulting for organizations setting up privacy-preserving collaboration networks. Our deep pharma experience with OpenDSU provides immediate credibility and a clear path to early customers in regulated industries that value proven expertise combined with cutting-edge technology.
