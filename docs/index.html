Investor Memo
Swiss Venture Studio + Romanian AI Lab

Research-born AI ventures for enterprise markets that need trust, auditability, and speed.

Outfinity is being formed in Switzerland as the venture studio that commercializes applied AI systems developed with Axiologic Research in Romania. The studio builds focused companies around validated technical assets, domain partners, and operators who can sell into serious enterprise workflows.

The goal is not to pitch generic autonomous agents. The goal is to translate research into products that help knowledge workers and regulated organizations use AI with clearer boundaries, better validation, and lower adoption risk.

Who This Is For

Different partners need different evidence.

Each audience should see the evidence that matters to them: venture economics, strategic access, research translation, operator fit, or technical substance.

Venture Capital

Looks for a repeatable company-creation engine, defensible technical assets, design-partner traction, and clear equity paths into spin-outs.

Relevant ask: platform round, studio stake, or spin-out seed lead.

Corporate and Strategic Investors

Need early access to trustworthy AI workflows without absorbing open-ended research risk or becoming locked into fragile prototypes.

Relevant ask: co-development, venture partnership, procurement bridge.

Public and Non-dilutive Capital

Fits the research translation layer: open components, evaluation methods, trustworthy AI tooling, and European digital infrastructure.

Relevant ask: R&D projects, consortium work, applied research funding.

Research Partners

Bring domain problems, validation settings, and scientific credibility. Outfinity helps turn results into products without confusing research with charity.

Relevant ask: joint labs, pilots, datasets, grant consortia.

Operators and Founders

Join when a problem is narrow enough to sell, the technical core is mature enough to reuse, and the venture can have its own accountable leadership.

Relevant ask: CEO/operator roles for focused spin-outs.

AI and Technology Public

Can follow the public research direction, open tooling, and practical debate around local, auditable, bounded AI systems.

Relevant ask: technical updates, demos, explainers, community review.
Why Now

The opportunity is in governed AI workflows, not AI theater.

Enterprises are moving from experimentation to operational adoption. That shift favors teams that can connect research, software architecture, validation, and go-to-market discipline.

01

AI must enter existing work, not replace it with a demo.

Useful products will embed into documents, research pipelines, data rooms, compliance checks, and expert review loops where mistakes have cost.

02

Regulated buyers need bounded systems.

Audit trails, explicit decomposition, validation gates, human approval, and local deployment options matter more than claims of full autonomy.

03

Research can become a venture pipeline.

Axiologic Research provides the lab surface; Outfinity packages mature capabilities into ventures with domain focus, operator ownership, and capital discipline.

Milestones Before a Large Equity Round

Publish the investable architecture.

Make the platform, licensing approach, lab/studio boundary, and candidate venture areas legible.

Secure design partners.

Prioritize regulated enterprise or research workflows where buyers can validate accuracy, governance, and ROI.

Package the first spin-outs.

Each venture needs a named market, operator profile, field-of-use rights, and measurable customer problem.

Raise around evidence.

Use equity when it accelerates a venture that already has technical leverage and a path to distribution.

Investor Fit

What a useful conversation should cover.

VC funds

Studio economics, first spin-out pipeline, founder/operator plan, early revenue milestones, and IP/licensing structure.

Corporate venture

Strategic access, pilot rights, procurement route, data boundaries, and whether a spin-out can serve the wider market.

Strategic customers

Workflow pain, deployment constraints, validation criteria, internal champion, and timeline to paid production use.

Research funders

Scientific contribution, public outputs, consortium partners, evaluation methodology, and credible path from research to use.
